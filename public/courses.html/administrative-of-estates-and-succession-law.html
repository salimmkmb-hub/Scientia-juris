Characteristics of Will
1. Posthumous effect, it means a will is a legal declaration of the intentions of a testator with respect to his property, which he desires to be carried into effect after his death. For any will take effect or start operating there are to be a death of the testator, in absence of the death of the testator a will cannot take effect or operate. it discussed in the case of Attorney General v. Jones Backley (1817) 3 Prince 368 In this case it state as the death of testator alone does not make the will effective. There must be express intention or Animus testandi in the will that, it should not operate until the death of the testator (maker). For example, "I gave and bequath unto my children X, Y and Z if they survive me". The rationale of expressing the intention in the will is to distinguish gift intervivo and gift upon death, the will can only take effect if there is an expressed wish intending the same to take effect upon or after death.
Also, in the case of Constantin Hamanya v. Elias Kayoza HCD 1968, 67 The plaintiff was successor in interest, by bequeath to a Shamba formerly owned by an old lady. Before her death, a descendant of the old lady, had incurred adebt to the first defendant, which he had honored by permitting his creditor to pick 100 debes of coffee from the shamba. The lady learned of this arrangement and gave her verbal approval. After er death, the plaintiff sued both defendants, in the Primary Court In Bukoba - District, for the value of the cofee removed from the Shamba. Mustafa, J. Held that; "Plaintiff was not the owner of the shamba until after the old lady's death, and therefore, has no right to the value of the coffee removed from it during her lifetime--- Plaintiff would be entitled to compersation for coffee picked subsequent to her death ..."
Case of Olivant v. Wright (1875) 1 Ch D 346, It is stated that, "... a will is an instrument which is not take effect till the death of the testator."
2. Revocable, it means a will can be revoked or cancelled by the testator at any time before his death. The testator may revoke his will by making a new will or by destroying the will with the intention of revoking it for the reason that when the testator is alive he has the right to change his mind and make a new will or destroy the old will. The testator may revoke his will by making a new will or by destroying the will with the intention of revoking it.

TYPES OF WILL
1. JOINT WILL - This is a will made up by two or more parties, who wish to merge their instructions into a single document that details what they want to have happen after they pass. Usually married couple make them, and they often specify that the first spouse who dies leave all money and propert to the other and then the property transfers to the couple's children or grand children after the death of the last surviving spouse. Once the first spouses died the instruments can no longer be changed, they become irrevocable.
2. TESTAMENTARY TRUST WILL - These create a special types of trust that is established after death. The person making the will names a trustee and beneficiaries and provides instructions on how the trustee should use trust assets, when and how they should be transferred to beneficiaries. This done in circumstance where someone leaves money or property to a minor child and wants to appoint a trustee to manage those assets on their behalf and ensure the fund are used appropriately.

3. MUTUAL WILL - Is a foundational document in estate planning, allowing an individual to direct the distribution of their property/ assets after death. This introduce a layer of contractual obligation into estate planning process for two peoples, altering how their assets can be managed and distributed. In Mutual will two individual create separate will based on an agreement about how their property will be distributed. The cental features is a binding contract where each person agrees to their will's terms in exchange for the other's promise to do the same. This agreement dictates that the terms of the will not be changed, especially after one of the individual has pass away, making the arrangement irrevocable upon the death of the first person.

4. CONDITIONAL WILL - Is a legal will that specifies how a person's assets will be distributed only if certain conditions are fulfilled. For examples, condition will might state "I leave my estate estate to my children if idie before my spouse" if the spouse survive's the testator, the will does not take effect and the estate may be distributed.

5. HOLOGRAPHIC WILL - It is handwritten will with no witnesssignature. The creator of will writers and signs a document providing instructions for the distribution of assets after death. While not accepted in all states, holographic will can be considered legal valid in many jurisdiction will is not applicable in Tanzania because it did not meet the requirement of valid will.
