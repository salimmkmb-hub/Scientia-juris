CRIMINAL LAW Sheria ya Makosa ya Jinai - Tanzania Mainland

General Principles of Criminal Liability & Defences

Topic 1: Elements of Crime (Actus Reus & Mens Rea)

For criminal liability to attach under common law principles and the Tanzanian Penal Code, two fundamental elements must concur: the physical act or omission (**Actus Reus**) and the mental element or guilty mind (**Mens Rea**).

📌 Legal Maxim:
"Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea" — The act itself does not make a person guilty unless the mind is also guilty.

1. Actus Reus (The Physical Element)

Refers to the unlawful act, omission, or state of affairs prohibited by law. It includes the conduct, surrounding circumstances, and the consequence of that conduct.

2. Mens Rea (The Mental Element)

Refers to the state of mind required for the commission of an offense, such as intention, recklessness, knowledge, or criminal negligence.

⚖️ Precedent Case: R v. Mohan [1976] QB 1
Mahakama ilieleza maana ya "Intention" (dhamira/dhamira ya wazi) kama nia au lengo kuu la mtuhumiwa kufikia matokeo yaliyopigwa marufuku na sheria, bila kujali iwapo alitaka matokeo hayo yatokee kwa sababu gani.

Topic 2: General Defences in Criminal Law

The Penal Code, Cap. 16 provides statutory general defences under Chapter IV that negate criminal liability or reduce the severity of the offense.

1. Insanity (Akili Isiyo Trazimamu) - Section 12 & 13

A person is not criminally responsible for an act or omission if at the time of doing the act, they were suffering from a disease affecting the mind which rendered them incapable of understanding what they were doing or knowing that it was wrong (M'Naghten Rules).

📌 Penal Code, Cap. 16 [R.E. 2019] - Section 12
"A person is not criminally responsible for an act or omission if at the time of doing the act or making the omission he be through any disease affecting his mind incapable of understanding what he is doing, or of knowing that he ought not to do the act or make the omission."

2. Intoxication (Ulevi) - Section 14

As a general rule, voluntary intoxication is not a defence. However, involuntary intoxication (where drugs/alcohol were administered without the person's consent or knowledge) or intoxication resulting in temporary insanity can form a valid defence.

3. Self-Defence & Defence of Property - Section 18 & 18A

A person is justified in using reasonable and proportionate force to defend themselves, another person, or property against imminent unlawful attack.

⚖️ Precedent Case: Palmer v. R [1971] AC 814
Mahakama ilifafanua kuwa mtu anayejibizana na shambulio la ghafla hawezi kupima kipimo cha nguvu anachotumia kwa ulaini, bali nguvu inayotumika lazima iwe ya kiasi cha msingi kulingana na hatari iliyopo (Proportionate Force).

Topic 3: Parties to Offences & Inchoate Crimes

Criminal liability extends beyond the principal offender who actually commits the actus reus.

1. Parties to Offences (Section 22)

  • Principal Offender: The person who actually executes the crime.
  • Aiders and Abettors: Persons who enable, aid, abet, counsel, or procure the commission of the offence.

2. Inchoate Crimes (Makosa Yasiyokamilika)

Crimes that are committed in preparation for or seeking to accomplish another crime:

  • Attempt (Subira/Kujaribu) - Section 380: An act done with intent to commit an offence, forming part of a series of acts which would constitute its actual commission if not interrupted.
  • Conspiracy (Njama) - Section 384: An agreement between two or more persons to commit an unlawful act.

Topic 4: Specific Offences (Homicide & Property Crimes)

1. Murder vs Manslaughter

  • Murder (Mauaji ya Kukusudia) - Section 196: Unlawful killing of a human being with malice aforethought (Section 200). Penalty: Death Sentence (Section 197).
  • Manslaughter (Mauaji Bila Kukusudia) - Section 195: Unlawful killing committed without malice aforethought (e.g., under sudden provocation - Section 201).
📌 Penal Code, Cap. 16 - Section 200 (Malice Aforethought)
Malice aforethought shall be deemed to be established by evidence proving an intention to cause death or grievous harm, or knowledge that the act will probably cause death.

2. Theft & Robbery

  • Theft (Wizi) - Section 258: Fraudulently and without claim of right taking and carrying away anything capable of being stolen with intent to permanently deprive the owner.
  • Robbery (Unyang'anyi wa Kutumia Nguvu) - Section 285: Theft accompanied by actual force or threat of violence to any person or property to obtain or retain the stolen property.
