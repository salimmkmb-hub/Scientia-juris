MINING & NATURAL RESOURCES LAW Sheria ya Madini na Maliasili - Tanzania Mainland

Ownership, Mineral Licensing, State Participation & Local Content

Topic 1: Ownership of Natural Resources & Permanent Sovereignty

In Tanzania, all mineral resources, natural wealth, and natural resources in, under, or upon any land are the public property of the Tanzanian people, vested in the President in trust for the nation.

📌 The Natural Wealth and Resources (Permanent Sovereignty) Act, 2017 - Section 4
"The Permanent Sovereignty of the United Republic over natural wealth and resources shall not be compromised, and the ownership of all natural wealth and resources is vested in the President on behalf of the People of the United Republic."

Key Principles Introduced by the 2017 Natural Resources Legal Reforms:

  • Prohibition of Exporting Raw Minerals: Raw minerals/ores must be processed, refined, or beneficiated within Tanzania before export.
  • Prohibition of Foreign Arbitration: Disputes arising from natural resource extraction must be resolved within domestic judicial or dispute resolution frameworks (Tanzanian courts or tribunals).
  • Review of Unconscionable Terms: Under the 2017 Re-negotiation Act, Parliament is empowered to review and renegotiate any contractual terms in natural resource agreements deemed unconscionable or biased against the State.

Topic 2: Regulatory Authority & Mineral Rights Licensing

The primary legislation governing mining operations is The Mining Act, Cap. 123. The sector is regulated by the Mining Commission (Tume ya Madini), established under Section 21 of the Act.

📌 The Mining Act, Cap. 123 [R.E. 2019] - Section 22
The Mining Commission is responsible for issuing, renewing, suspending, and revoking mineral rights, regulating mining operations, enforcing environmental compliance, and auditing mineral production.

Categories of Mineral Rights / Licenses

  • Prospecting License (PL): Grants exclusive rights to search and prospect for minerals within a specified geographic area.
  • Special Mining License (SML): Granted for large-scale mining operations involving capital investment exceeding USD 100 Million.
  • Mining License (ML): Granted for medium-scale operations with investment between USD 100,000 and USD 100 Million.
  • Primary Mining License (PML): Reserved exclusively for Tanzanian citizens or Tanzanian-owned entities engaged in small-scale mining.

Topic 3: Government Free Carried Interest & Royalty Framework

To ensure maximum public economic benefits from non-renewable mineral resources, Tanzanian law requires statutory state participation and mineral royalties.

1. Government Free Carried Interest

📌 The Mining Act, Cap. 123 - Section 10
"In any mining operations under a Special Mining License or Mining License, the Government shall have a non-dilutable free carried interest of not less than 16% in the capital of the mining company..."

In addition, the Government holds an option to acquire up to 50% total shares in mining entities proportional to the value of tax incentives granted.

2. Royalty and Inspection Fees (Section 87)

Royalties are payable to the Government on the gross value of minerals produced:

  • Gemstones & Diamond: 6% Royalty rate.
  • Metallic Minerals (Gold, Copper, Silver): 6% Royalty rate.
  • Building Materials & Industrial Minerals: 3% Royalty rate.
  • Clearance & Inspection Fee: Statutory 1% inspection fee on the gross value of all exported minerals.

Topic 4: Local Content & Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)

Part VIII of the Mining Act, along with the Mining (Local Content) Regulations, establishes strict obligations for mining companies to integrate local capacity and community development into their operations.

Mandatory Local Content & CSR Provisions:

  • Employment & Training: Mining licensees must give preference to qualified Tanzanian citizens and implement succession plans for foreign technical roles.
  • Procurement of Goods & Services: Preference must be given to goods and services produced or provided by Tanzanian-owned firms or joint ventures with local equity participation.
  • CSR Strategy (Section 105): Mining license holders must prepare and execute an annual Corporate Social Responsibility plan agreed upon jointly with local government authorities in the operational area.
