Intellectual Property Rights, Protection & Enforcement

Topic 1: Nature, Foundations & Branches of Intellectual Property

Intellectual Property (IP) refers to creations of the mind—such as inventions, literary and artistic works, designs, symbols, names, and images used in commerce. Unlike real property (land) or personal property (goods), IP represents intangible property rights protected by law to enable creators to earn recognition or financial benefit from what they invent or create.

Primary Branches of Intellectual Property Law:

  • Copyright and Neighbouring Rights: Protects original literary, artistic, dramatic, musical works, and computer programs (software).
  • Industrial Property: Covers Patents (inventions), Trademarks (brand names/logos), Industrial Designs, Trade Secrets, and Geographical Indications (GI).
📌 Regulatory Institutions in Tanzania:
1. COSOTA (Copyright Society of Tanzania): Administers copyright registration and collective rights management under Cap. 218.
2. BRELA (Business Registrations and Licensing Agency): Administers registration of Patents, Trademarks, and Trade Names.

Topic 2: Copyright & Neighbouring Rights Law

Copyright protects the expression of ideas, not the idea itself (Idea-Expression Dichotomy). Under Tanzanian law, copyright protection arises automatically upon creation and expression of the work in a tangible form, provided the work is original.

1. Requirements for Copyright Protection

  • Originality: The work must originate from the author through intellectual effort, creativity, or skill.
  • Fixation: The work must be expressed in a material or tangible form (written down, recorded, filmed, or coded).
📌 Copyright and Neighbouring Rights Act, Cap. 218 [R.E. 2019] - Section 4(1)
"Authors of original literary and artistic works shall be entitled to copyright protection upon creation of their works without formal registration requirements."

2. Rights Conferred on Copyright Holders

Copyright comprises two distinct sets of rights:

  • Economic Rights (Section 9): Exclusive right to reproduce, publish, distribute, perform, broadcast, or adapt the work. These rights can be assigned, licensed, or sold.
  • Moral Rights (Section 11): Right to claim authorship (Paternity Right) and the right to object to any distortion or mutilation of the work that harms the author's reputation (Integrity Right). Moral rights remain with the author even after assigning economic rights.

Duration of Protection (Section 14): Protection lasts for the life of the author plus 50 years after their death.

⚖️ Precedent Case: Donoghue v. Allied Newspapers Ltd [1938] Ch 106

Topic 3: Patents & Inventions Law

A Patent is an exclusive legal right granted by the state for an invention—a technical solution to a specific technological problem. A patent grants the inventor exclusive rights to prevent others from commercially exploiting the invention for a limited period (typically 20 years).

📌 Patents Act, Cap. 217 [R.E. 2019] - Section 8
An invention is patentable if it satisfies three cumulative legal tests:
1. Novelty: The invention must be new and not part of the prior art anywhere in the world.
2. Inventive Step (Non-obviousness): It must not be obvious to a person skilled in the relevant art.
3. Industrial Applicability: It must be capable of being made or used in any kind of industry.

Non-Patentable Subject Matter (Section 7): Discoveries, scientific theories, mathematical methods, plant or animal varieties, and methods for treatment of the human or animal body by surgery or therapy.

Topic 4: Trademarks & Brand Protection

A Trademark is a distinctive sign, logo, word, symbol, or combination used by a business to identify its goods or services and distinguish them from competitors in the market.

📌 Trade and Service Marks Act, Cap. 326 [R.E. 2019] - Section 16
"A trade or service mark shall be registered if it is capable of distinguishing goods or services with which the applicant is or may be connected in the course of trade..."

Infringement vs. Passing Off

  • Trademark Infringement: Occurs when a person uses an identical or deceptively similar mark on similar goods without authorization, violating a registered trademark under Cap. 326.
  • Action for Passing Off: A common law remedy used to protect unregistered brand names or trade dress against misrepresentation that damages goodwill.
⚖️ Precedent Case: Reckitt & Colman Products Ltd v. Borden Inc (1990) 1 WLR 491 (Jif Lemon Case)
Kesi hii ilieleza vipengele vitatu vya msingi vya kuthibitisha shauri la Passing Off (The Classical Trinity):
1. Kuwepo kwa jina zuri au sifa ya kibiashara (Goodwill / Reputation).
2. Udanganyifu au utatanishi kwa wateja (Misrepresentation).
3. Athari au hasara ya kifedha kwa mmiliki halisi (Damage / Loss).

Topic 5: Enforcement of IP Rights & Remedies

When an IP right is infringed, the right holder can institute civil or criminal proceedings in a court of competent jurisdiction (such as the Commercial Division of the High Court of Tanzania).

Legal Remedies for IP Infringement:

  • Injunctions: Temporary or permanent court orders restraining the infringing party from further unauthorized use or sale.
  • Damages or Account of Profits: Monetary compensation for financial losses suffered or recovery of illegal profits made by the infringer.
  • Seizure & Anton Piller Orders: Court orders allowing ex-parte search and seizure of counterfeit goods or evidence to prevent destruction before trial.
  • Delivery Up or Destruction: Court orders directing the surrender or destruction of infringing/counterfeit materials.
