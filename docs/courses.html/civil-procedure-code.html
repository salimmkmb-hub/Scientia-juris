PRINCIPLE OF RES-SUBJUDICE
This is the latin word which means matter under judgment, is when a matter is said subjudice when is under trial or pending before compent court. The principle od res-subjudice provides that two different case if treated in different court it shall not decided at per or at sametime. So, the subsequent case should be or should wait for the previous case to be determine section 8 of Civil Procedure Code, Cap 33, R.E 2023
Elements of Res-subjudice
i. Matter in issue in the suit/ in the second suit must be direct or sub-stential the same as the first suit. In the case of Ibrahim S. kajembo v. The Commissioner of Land and two others
ii. Parties in the second suit must be the same or both of them are claiming the same title in the first suit
In the case of Wanget Windrose Safari (T) ltd v. The Minister of Nature resources and Tourism and two others, it was observed that "the sub-stentiality and directness of the suit are not to be determine based on remedy sought in either the suit but on the subject matter" When the elements for the defence or claims are dentical the same should be held to be direct and substentiality in issue and doctrine to support sub-judice.
iii. The court will use previous (first) suit to institute second suit is competent to grant the relief claim in subsequent suit. It is not necessary that a particulary matter may be instituted in every court because every court has ot is own jurisdiction.
iv. The previous instituted case must be pending

    CIVIL PROCEDURE CODE Sheria ya Mwenendo wa Mashauri ya Madai - Tanzania Mainland

    Pleadings, Court Process, Trial Procedure & Execution

    Topic 1: Institution of Suits & Pleadings (Plaint & Written Statement of Defence)

    Civil litigation in Tanzania is governed primarily by The Civil Procedure Code (CPC), Cap. 33. Every suit is instituted by presenting a Plaint to the court or in such other manner as may be prescribed.

    📌 Civil Procedure Code, Cap. 33 [R.E. 2019] - Section 22 & Order IV Rule 1
    "Every suit shall be instituted by presenting a plaint to the court or such officer as it appoints in this behalf. Every plaint shall comply with the rules contained in Orders VI and VII, so far as they are applicable."

    1. The Plaint (Hati ya Mashtaka ya Madai) - Order VII

    The statement of claim filed by the Plaintiff. It must contain the court name, details of parties, facts constituting the cause of action, jurisdiction statement, and the specific relief sought.

    2. Written Statement of Defence (WSD) - Order VIII

    Upon service of summons, the Defendant must file a Written Statement of Defence (WSD) within the prescribed statutory period (usually 21 days). Any allegation of fact in the plaint not denied specifically shall be taken to be admitted.

    ⚖️ Precedent Case: Blay v. Pollard and Morris [1930] 1 KB 628
    Kesi hii inafundisha kanuni ya msingi ya Pleadings: Mahakama lazima iamue shauri kulingana na hoja zilizowasilishwa kwenye Plaint na WSD pekee. Pande haziruhusiwi kuleta ushahidi wa vitu ambavyo havikuandikwa kwenye Pleadings bila kufanya marekebisho (Amendment of Pleadings under Order VI Rule 17).

    Topic 2: Interlocutory Applications (Chamber Summons & Affidavits)

    During the pendency of a suit, parties may apply for temporary reliefs (e.g., temporary injunctions, extension of time, or striking out pleadings) through interlocutory applications under Order XLIII.

    📌 Order XLIII Rule 2 - Chamber Summons & Affidavits
    Interlocutory applications are generally brought by way of Chamber Summons supported by an Affidavit (a sworn written statement of facts verified on oath).

    Key Rules on Affidavits (Order XIX):

    • An affidavit must be confined only to facts that the deponent is able of his own knowledge to prove.
    • On interlocutory applications, statements as to belief may be admitted provided the sources and grounds thereof are stated.
    • An defective or false affidavit is liable to be struck out by the court.

    Topic 3: Pre-Trial Proceedings & Trial (First Hearing & Scheduling)

    To ensure speedy disposition of commercial and civil cases, the CPC incorporates mandatory pre-trial mechanisms.

    • First Hearing & Mediation (Order VIIIA / VIIIB): Courts conduct Speed Management and mandatory Mediation sessions to explore out-of-court settlements before full trial.
    • Framing of Issues (Order XIV): If mediation fails, the court frames issues of fact and law arising from the conflict between Plaint and WSD.
    • Ex-Parte Proceedings (Order IX Rule 6): Where the Defendant fails to appear or file WSD despite proof of service, the court may proceed to hear the suit ex-parte and pass judgment.

    Topic 4: Judgments, Decrees & Execution (Order XXI)

    Once the court hears evidence and arguments, it pronounces its Judgment, upon which a formal document called a Decree is drawn up.

    📌 Execution of Decrees - Section 38 & Order XXI
    The holder of a decree (Decree-Holder) can apply to the court to enforce and execute the decree against the Judgment-Debtor.

    Modes of Execution (Section 38):

    • By delivery of any property specifically decreed.
    • By attachment and sale, or sale without attachment, of any property of the judgment-debtor.
    • By arrest and detention in civil prison of the judgment-debtor.
    • By appointing a Receiver.
