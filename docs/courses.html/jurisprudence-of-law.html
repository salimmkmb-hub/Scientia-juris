Nature, Theories, and Concepts of Jurisprudence

Topic 1: Nature & Meaning of Jurisprudence

Jurisprudence is broadly defined as the study, knowledge, and philosophy of law. Unlike core substantive laws (like Criminal Law or Contract Law) which state what the legal rules are, Jurisprudence examines why law exists, its relation to justice and morality, and how legal systems function.

Primary Objectives of Jurisprudence:

  • Analyzing the nature of law, legal concepts, and legal systems.
  • Understanding the relationship between law, morality, power, and society.
  • Evaluating the validity and source of legal authority.

Topic 2: Major Schools of Jurisprudence

Legal philosophy is divided into distinct schools of thought that explain the source and validity of law differently:

1. Natural Law School (Lex Injusta Non Est Lex)

Presents the view that law is derived from universal moral values, reason, or divine authority. Advocates argue that an unjust law is no law at all. Key thinkers include Thomas Aquinas, Aristotle, and John Locke.

📌 Natural Law Maxim (St. Thomas Aquinas)
"Lex injusta non est lex" — An unjust law is not a law. Law must conform to reason, morality, and human rights.

2. Legal Positivism (Analytical School)

Rejects the necessary connection between law and morality. Legal Positivists assert that law is simply a system of rules created by a sovereign political authority ("law as it is" vs. "law as it ought to be"). Key thinkers include John Austin, Jeremy Bentham, and H.L.A. Hart.

📌 John Austin's Command Theory
"Law is the command of a sovereign, backed by the threat of a sanction, and habitually obeyed."

3. Marxist Theory of Law & State

Views law not as a neutral set of rules, but as an instrument of class rule used by the ruling class (bourgeoisie) to control economic resources and suppress the working class (proletariat). Law changes as economic structures evolve.

4. Historical & Realist Schools

  • Historical School (Savigny): Law is a product of human customs, national character, and historical evolution (Volksgeist).
  • American & Scandinavian Legal Realism (Oliver Wendell Holmes): Law is what judges actually decide in courts, not merely abstract written statutes.
⚖️ Precedent / Philosophical Landmark: The Nuremberg Trials (1945–1946)

Topic 3: Key Concepts of Jurisprudence

Jurisprudence investigates fundamental legal terms that form the building blocks of legal reasoning:

  • Rights and Duties: A legal right is an interest recognized and protected by law, which imposes a corresponding legal duty on others.
  • Legal Personality: The status of an entity recognized by law as having rights and obligations (Natural persons vs. Corporate entities/Artificial persons).
  • Ownership and Possession: Ownership is the ultimate legal right over a thing, whereas Possession represents physical control coupled with the intention to hold it (animus possidendi).
