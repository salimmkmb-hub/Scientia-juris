BUSINESS ASSOCIATION LAW Sheria ya Makampuni na Vyama vya Kibiashara - Tanzania Mainland

Corporate Personality, Company Formation, Governance & Winding Up

Topic 1: Types of Business Associations & Corporate Personality

Business Association Law governs the legal structures through which commercial enterprises operate. The main forms of business associations include Sole Proprietorships, Partnerships, and Registered Companies.

1. Sole Proprietorships & Partnerships

  • Sole Proprietorship: An unincorporated business owned by one individual. There is no legal separation between the owner and the business entity.
  • Partnership: Governed by the Law of Contract Act, Cap. 345 (Part XI). It is the relation which subsists between persons carrying on a business in common with a view of profit. Partners have unlimited joint and several liability.

2. The Doctrine of Corporate Personality

Upon incorporation under the Companies Act, Cap. 212, a company becomes a separate legal entity distinct from its shareholders and directors.

📌 Companies Act, Cap. 212 [R.E. 2019] - Section 15
"From the date of incorporation mentioned in the certificate of incorporation, the subscribers of the memorandum... shall be a body corporate by the name contained in the memorandum, capable forthwith of exercising all the functions of an incorporated company..."
⚖️ Precedent Case: Salomon v. A. Salomon & Co. Ltd [1897] AC 22
Kesi iliyoweka msingi mkuu wa sheria za makampuni duniani (*Separate Legal Entity*). Baraza la Mabwana (House of Lords) liliamua kuwa kampuni ikishasajiliwa inakuwa na nafsi tofauti kisheria na wamiliki wake, hata kama mtu mmoja anamiliki karibu hisa zote.

Lifting the Corporate Veil

The courts or statutes may exceptionally "pierce or lift the corporate veil" to ignore separate legal personality and hold shareholders or directors personally liable in cases of fraud, tax evasion, trading while insolvent, or sham companies.

Topic 2: Formation, Constitutional Documents & Membership

A company is formed by registering prescribed statutory documents with the **Business Registrations and Licensing Agency (BRELA)**.

Key Constitutional Documents:

  • Memorandum of Association (MOA): Governs the company’s external relations. It outlines the company name, registered office, objects clause, limitation of liability, and share capital structure.
  • Articles of Association (AOA): Governs the internal administration and regulations of the company (e.g., voting rights, appointment of directors, transfer of shares, and board meeting procedures).
📌 Doctrine of Ultra Vires in Company Law (Section 34)
Historically, acts done by a company outside the scope of its objects clause in the Memorandum were void. Under Section 34 of Cap. 212, the validity of an act done by a company shall not be called into question on the ground of lack of capacity by reason of anything in the memorandum.

Topic 3: Company Governance & Fiduciary Duties of Directors

Directors are agents and trustees appointed by shareholders to manage the business and affairs of the company.

📌 Companies Act, Cap. 212 - Section 182 to 185 (Directors' Statutory Duties)
Directors owe strict fiduciary duties and duties of care, skill, and diligence to the company.

Core Fiduciary Duties of Directors:

  • Duty to Act in Good Faith: Directors must act honestly in what they consider to be the best interests of the company as a whole.
  • Duty to Avoid Conflict of Interest: Directors must not place themselves in a position where their personal interests conflict with the company's interests (Section 209 requires disclosure of personal interest in contracts).
  • Duty of Care and Skill: Directors must exercise reasonable care, skill, and diligence expected of a person carrying out their functions.
  • Duty Not to Make Secret Profits: Directors must account to the company for any corporate opportunity or profit derived from their position.
⚖️ Precedent Case: Regal (Hastings) Ltd v. Gulliver [1942] 1 All ER 378

Topic 4: Winding Up & Dissolution of Companies

Winding up (Liquidation) is the legal process by which the operations of a company are brought to an end, its assets collected and realized, its liabilities paid off, and any surplus distributed among shareholders.

Modes of Winding Up under Cap. 212 (Section 272):

  1. Compulsory Winding Up (by the High Court): Instituted via a court petition under statutory grounds such as inability to pay debts (Insolvency), or where the court is of the opinion that it is just and equitable to wind up the company.
  2. Voluntary Winding Up: Initiated by resolution of the members/shareholders (Members' Voluntary Winding Up) or creditors (Creditors' Voluntary Winding Up).
  3. Winding Up under Court Supervision: Conducted voluntarily but monitored by court orders.
