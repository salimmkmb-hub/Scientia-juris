LAW OF EVIDENCE Sheria ya Ushahidi - Tanzania Mainland

Principles & Framework of Law of Evidence

Topic 1: Relevancy & Admissibility of Facts

The Law of Evidence governs how facts are proved in court. The general rule is that evidence may only be given of facts in issue and relevant facts. Relevancy is determined by logic and legal principles, whereas admissibility is a rule of law—not all relevant facts are admissible if they are excluded by specific legal rules.

📌 Law of Evidence Act, Cap. 6 [R.E. 2019] - Section 7
"Evidence may be given in any suit or proceeding of the existence or non-existence of every fact in issue and of such other facts as are hereinafter declared to be relevant, and of no others."

Fact in Issue: Means any fact from which, either by itself or in connection with other facts, the existence, non-existence, nature, or extent of any right, liability, or disability asserted or denied in any suit or proceeding necessarily follows.

Topic 2: Oral & Documentary Evidence

Evidence presented before the court is broadly categorized into Oral Evidence (statements made by witnesses in court) and Documentary Evidence (documents produced for the inspection of the court).

1. Oral Evidence & Direct Evidence Rule

All facts, except the contents of documents, may be proved by oral evidence. As a general principle, oral evidence must in all cases whatever be direct (meaning a person who saw, heard, or perceived it must testify himself/herself).

📌 Law of Evidence Act, Cap. 6 - Section 62(1)
"Oral evidence must, in all cases whatever, be direct..."

2. Primary vs Secondary Documentary Evidence

Documents must be proved by primary evidence except in specified cases where secondary evidence is admissible.

  • Primary Evidence (Section 64): The document itself produced for the inspection of the court.
  • Secondary Evidence (Section 65): Certified copies, copies made from the original by mechanical processes, or oral accounts of the contents of a document.
⚖️ Precedent Case: Trust Bank Tanzania Ltd v. Le-Mechant Ltd & Others (2002)
Mahakama ilisisitiza matumizi ya msingi wa ushahidi wa nyaraka (Documentary Evidence) na mazingira ambapo ushahidi wa sekondari (Secondary Evidence) au ushahidi wa kielektroniki unaweza kupokewa mahakamani pindi nyaraka ya asili inapokuwa haipatikani.

Topic 3: Burden of Proof & Onus of Proof

The Burden of Proof refers to the legal obligation of a party to prove an allegation or charge in court.

1. Standard of Proof (Kiwango cha Ushahidi)

  • In Criminal Cases: Proof beyond reasonable doubt (Kuthibitisha bila kuacha shaka yoyote inayojenga msingi).
  • In Civil Cases: Proof on a balance of probabilities (Kiwango cha uzito wa ushahidi unaoelekea ukweli).
📌 Law of Evidence Act, Cap. 6 - Section 110(1)
"Whoever desires any court to give judgment as to any legal right or liability dependent on the existence of facts which he asserts, must prove that those facts exist."
⚖️ Precedent Case: Woolmington v. DPP [1935] AC 462
Msingi mkuu wa sheria ya jinai unaoeleza kuwa jukumu la kuthibitisha makosa ya jinai ni la upande wa mashtaka (Prosecution) kuanzia mwanzo hadi mwisho wa shauri ("Golden Thread of Criminal Law").
