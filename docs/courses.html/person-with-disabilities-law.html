PERSONS WITH DISABILITIES LAW Sheria ya Haki za Watu Wenye Ulemavu - Tanzania Mainland

Rights, Non-Discrimination, Accessibility, Employment & Governance

Topic 1: Constitutional Foundations & International Legal Framework

Disability rights law in Tanzania moves away from the historical medical/charity model to a rights-based approach. This framework guarantees that persons with disabilities enjoy full human rights, equality, and human dignity without discrimination.

📌 Constitutional Basis - Article 13 of the 1977 Constitution of Tanzania
All persons are equal before the law and are entitled, without any discrimination, to equal protection and equal benefit of the law.

International & Regional Instruments:

  • UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (UNCRPD, 2006): Ratified by Tanzania in 2009, setting global standards for non-discrimination, accessibility, and legal capacity.
  • African Charter on Human and Peoples' Rights: Emphasizes the protection of vulnerable groups and elderly or disabled citizens.

Topic 2: Fundamental Principles of Cap. 183 & Rights Protection

The primary legislation governing disability rights in Tanzania Mainland is The Persons with Disabilities Act, Cap. 183 [R.E. 2019].

📌 The Persons with Disabilities Act, Cap. 183 - Section 4 & 5
Prohibits all forms of discrimination on the ground of disability and guarantees the rights to respect, human dignity, liberty, and protection from inhuman or degrading treatment.

Core Statutory Rights under Cap. 183:

  • Right to Life and Human Dignity: Absolute protection against harm, abandonment, or cultural violence (e.g., attacks on persons with albinism).
  • Right to Live in the Community: Equal choice of residence and full integration into social life.
  • Equal Protection before the Law: Guarantee of effective legal aid, accessible court premises, and sign language interpreters in legal proceedings.

Topic 3: Education, Employment Quotas & Reasonable Accommodation

Cap. 183 enforces mandatory requirements on both public and private entities to foster inclusive education and fair employment.

1. Inclusive Education (Sections 27 - 30)

Educational institutions must provide inclusive education, accessible learning materials, adaptive technologies, and sign language options to ensure students with disabilities are not excluded from primary, secondary, or tertiary education.

2. Employment & Statutory Quota System (Sections 31 - 34)

📌 Statutory Employment Quota - Section 31(1)
"Every employer who employs twenty or more employees shall employ persons with disabilities to the extent of at least 3 percent of the total number of employees."

3. Reasonable Accommodation (Urekebishaji Unaostahili)

Employers and service providers are legally obligated to make necessary modifications or adjustments (reasonable accommodation) to work environments or facilities to enable a person with a disability to perform their duties, unless doing so imposes an undue hardship.

Topic 4: Accessibility, Physical Environment & Governance

To ensure independence and full participation, public infrastructure and information platforms must be accessible to all persons.

1. Physical Accessibility & Information Access (Sections 50 - 55)

  • Building Regulations: All public buildings, health facilities, schools, and transport terminals must feature ramps, wide doorways, accessible toilets, and tactile paving.
  • Public Transport: Transport operators must provide accessible vehicles and priority seating for persons with disabilities.
  • Information Access: Public broadcasts, news, and official announcements must incorporate sign language interpretation and accessible digital formats.

2. Institutional Framework: National Advisory Council

📌 The National Advisory Council for Persons with Disabilities (Section 9)
Established to advise the Minister, monitor compliance with Cap. 183, promote research, advocate for disability-inclusive budgeting, and safeguard the welfare of persons with disabilities nationwide.
