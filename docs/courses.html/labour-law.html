Topic 1: Definition & Meaning of Labour Law

Labour Law is the system of laws that defines the rights and responsibilities of workers and employers. It constitutes the body of statutes, administrative rulings, and judicial precedents that regulate the legal rights, restrictions, and working relationships among employees, employers, employer associations, and trade unions.

It governs essential employment matters including terms of employment, minimum wages, working conditions, health and safety, trade union operations, and labour-management relations.

Categories of Labour Law

  • Collective Labour Law: Regulates the tripartite and bipartite relationships between employers, employer associations, trade unions, and the State.
  • Individual Labour Law: Regulates the direct contractual relationship, rights, and duties existing between an individual employee and their employer.

Classification of Labour Law

  • Substantive Labour Law: Defines the actual legal rights, duties, and obligations of parties in a labour relationship (e.g., the Employment and Labour Relations Act [Cap. 400 R.E. 2019]).
  • Procedural Labour Law: Establishes the machinery, processes, and mechanisms for enforcing legal rights and resolving labour disputes (e.g., the Labour Institutions Act [Cap. 300 R.E. 2019] and the Public Service Act dispute settlement frameworks).

Topic 2: Sources of Labour Law in Tanzania

  1. The Constitution of the United Republic of Tanzania (CURT), 1977:

    Key provisions safeguarding fundamental rights and work-related principles include Articles 13 (Equality before the law), 14, 20 (Freedom of Association), 22 (Right to work), 23 (Right to fair remuneration), and 25 (Duty to work).

  2. Statutory Law (Principal & Subsidiary Legislation):
    • Employment and Labour Relations Act (ELRA) [Cap. 400 R.E. 2019]
    • Labour Institutions Act (LIA) [Cap. 300 R.E. 2019]
    • Public Service Act [Cap. 298 R.E. 2019]
    • Workers' Compensation Act [Cap. 263 R.E. 2015]
    • Law of Contract Act [Cap. 345 R.E. 2019]
  3. Judicial Precedents / Case Law:

    Applies directly, particularly where statutes are silent or require interpretation. Key illustrative cases include:

    • Mbeya Regional Trading Co. Ltd. v. Twiha Syego Mwaigomole (1988)
    • McClelland v. Northern Ireland General Health Services Board [1957] 1 All ER 129
    • JUWATA v. EHTU KIUTA
    • Paul Revocatus Kaunda v. The Attorney General (Civil Appeal 5 of 2018)
  4. Received Law: Application of Common Law doctrines and principles of equity pursuant to Section 2(2) of the Judicature and Application of Laws Act (JALA) [Cap. 358 R.E. 2019].
  5. Juristic Writings & Scholarly Literature: Persuasive legal texts and academic treatises written by legal scholars.
  6. Contract of Employment: Individual agreements entered into by parties setting out specific rights, obligations, and working conditions.
  7. Collective Bargaining Agreements (CBAs): Formally negotiated agreements between an employer (or employer association) and a recognized trade union.
  8. International Instruments: International Labour Organization (ILO) Conventions and Recommendations ratified by Tanzania.

Topic 3: Evolution & Historical Background of Labour Law

Prerequisites for Wage Labour

Wage labour replaces a basic natural (subsistence) economy through specific socio-economic developments:

  1. Development of a commodity economy over a subsistence/natural economy.
  2. Transformation of human capacity to work into a market commodity exchanged for capital/money.
  3. Existence of legally free labour (prohibiting forced labour, as anchored under Section 6 of the ELRA).

Historical Phases in Tanzania

  • Colonial Era: Under German and British rule, labour laws were introduced primarily to serve colonial economic interests, secure cheap native labour, and discipline the working class. Statutory measures included forced labour, taxation, and recruitment networks (e.g., SILABU).
  • Post-Independence Era: After 1961, Tanzania inherited several colonial statutes such as the Employment Ordinance (Cap. 366) and the Regulation of Wages and Terms of Employment Ordinance (Cap. 300).
  • Arusha Declaration (1967) & 1980s: Enactment of socialist-oriented labour statutes including the Security of Employment Act (Cap. 574) and the Wages and Salaries General Revision Act of 1974.
  • Modern Reform Period (2004–Present): Consolidated legal reforms leading to the enactment of the ELRA and LIA in 2004 (effective 2006) to align national legislation with international standards.

Functions of Labour Law

  • Protecting employees by regulating termination of employment and setting minimum wages (e.g., Mh. Choligali case, 2008).
  • Regulating working conditions, hours of work, and employee health, safety, and welfare.
  • Balancing conflict of interest between employers and employees through clear definition of rights and duties.
  • Facilitating peaceful resolution of trade and individual labour disputes.
  • Fostering economic productivity and protecting workers' freedom of association.

Topic 4: Scope of Application & Contractual Classifications

Scope of Application

Under Section 2 of the ELRA, the Act applies to all employees and employers in both the private and public sectors within mainland Tanzania. However, public service employees are additionally governed by specific statutory schemes:

  • Public Service Act [Cap. 298]
  • Public Service Negotiating Machinery Act
  • Public Service Regulations (e.g., PSR 2022)
Legal Precedent on Statutory Conflict: In Attorney General v. Chama cha Walimu Tanzania (CWT), the Court (Mandia, J.) analyzed contradictions between public service laws and general labour laws, observing that specific public service negotiating machinery lacked certain dispute remedies, necessitating recourse to Standing Orders.

Contract of Service vs. Contract for Service

Parameter Contract of Service (Employee) Contract for Service (Independent Contractor)
Vicarious Liability Employer is generally liable for torts committed by the employee in the course of employment. Principal is generally not liable for actions of an independent contractor.
Health & Safety Employer owes a strict statutory and common law duty of care. Standard of care depends on specific contract terms; minimal statutory duty.
Taxation Employer deducts Pay As You Earn (PAYE) at source. Contractor accounts for their own taxes (e.g., Withholding Tax/VAT).
Social Security Mandatory statutory contributions (e.g., 15% employer, 5% employee). No mandatory employer social security contribution.
Statutory Rights Entitled to statutory benefits (leave, severance pay, notice, unfair dismissal protections). Not entitled to statutory employment benefits under the ELRA.

Parties & Non-Standard Contracts

Under Section 4 of the ELRA, an employee is defined as an individual who has entered into a contract of employment or any other contract to perform work personally. Modern work practices (outsourcing, subcontracting, gig work) have led to non-standard ("atypical") work arrangements. In response, ILO instruments (such as the Part-Time Work Convention No. 175 of 1994) and national laws set protections to ensure non-standard workers receive proportional benefits and protection.

Types of Employment Contracts (Section 14, ELRA)

  • Contract for an unspecified period of time (Permanent contract).
  • Contract for a specified period of time (Fixed-term contract).
  • Contract for a specific task.

Reference Case: People and Wildlife v. Debora Oswald (interpreting fixed-term contracts and Rule 11 of the Employment and Labour Relations Code of Good Practice, 2007).

Topic 5: Formation, Duties, and Terms of Employment

Formation & Written Particulars

While employment contracts can be oral or written, contracts must comply with Section 6 of the ELRA (free consent). Section 15 of the ELRA mandates that an employer must provide written particulars of employment to an employee upon commencement of service.

Implied Terms in Contracts of Employment

Apart from express terms agreed upon by parties, employment contracts carry implied statutory and common law terms. Under Section 13(2) of the ELRA, statutory minimum standards automatically read into employment agreements.

Duties of the Employer

  • Duty to receive the employee into service upon conclusion of the contract.
  • Duty to pay remuneration/wages in the agreed manner (Section 27, ELRA; Article 23, CURT).
  • Duty to provide safe working conditions and suitable tools (Section 98(2), ELRA).
  • Duty to honor statutory duties and respect the employee's dignity.
  • Duty to indemnify the employee for expenses or liabilities lawfully incurred on the employer's behalf.

Duties of the Employee

  • Duty to render personal service and attend work punctually.
  • Duty of obedience to lawful and reasonable orders within the scope of employment.
  • Duty to exercise due care, competence, and reasonable skill.
  • Duty of fidelity, good faith, loyalty, and confidentiality (protecting trade secrets).
  • Duty to give proper statutory or contractual notice of termination.

Topic 6: Fundamental Rights & Employment Standards (Part II, ELRA)

International Core Labour Standards (CLS)

  1. Freedom of Association and the effective recognition of the right to collective bargaining (ILO Conventions 87 & 98).
  2. Elimination of all forms of forced or compulsory labour (ILO Conventions 29 & 105; Section 6, ELRA).
  3. Effective abolition of child labour (ILO Conventions 138 & 182; Section 5, ELRA).
  4. Elimination of discrimination in respect of employment and occupation (ILO Conventions 100 & 111; Section 7, ELRA).

Statutory Rights under the ELRA

  • Prohibition of Child Labour (Section 5): Absolute prohibition of employment of children under 14 years. Children aged 14 to 17 may only perform light work that is not harmful to their health, development, or education. Employment of children under 18 in hazardous settings (mines, factories) is strictly prohibited.
  • Prohibition of Forced Labour (Section 6): Prohibits forced or bonded labour. Exclusions include military service under the National Defence Act, civic obligations, court-convicted penal work, and work during national emergencies.
  • Prohibition of Discrimination (Section 7): Prohibits direct or indirect discrimination based on nationality, race, tribe, political opinion, sex, or disability. Exceptions include affirmative action, inherent requirements of the job, and national citizenship preferences.
  • Freedom of Association (Section 9): Guarantees the right of employees and employers to form or join trade unions or employers' associations (e.g., Association of Tanzania Employers - ATE).

Statutory Leave Entitlements

  • Annual Leave (Section 31): Minimum 28 consecutive days on full pay in a 12-month leave cycle.
  • Maternity Leave (Section 33): 84 days for a single child; 100 days for twins/multiple births.
  • Paternity Leave (Section 34): 3 days within a leave cycle, provided the child is the employee's.
  • Sick Leave (Section 32): Maximum 126 days within a 36-month cycle (first 63 days on full pay, remaining 63 days on half pay).
  • Compassionate Leave (Section 34): Minimum 4 days for death of a spouse, child, parent, or immediate family member.

Topic 7: Termination of Employment (Part IV, ELRA)

Grounds for Termination (Section 37)

Termination of employment by an employer is lawful and fair only if it is based on valid reasons and conducted through fair procedure:

  • Employee Misconduct: Violation of established workplace codes of conduct.
  • Incapacity: Poor work performance, ill health, or injury.
  • Incompatibility: Inability to maintain harmonious workplace relationships.
  • Operational Requirements: Retrenchment/redundancy (governed by Section 38).

Procedural Fairness in Misconduct Cases

  1. Investigation into allegations and formal notification of charges to the employee at least 48 hours prior to the hearing.
  2. Providing the employee an opportunity to present a defense and be represented.
  3. Fair and impartial evaluation of evidence before issuing disciplinary penalties.
  4. Providing right of internal appeal or dispute referral.

Statutory Remedies for Unfair Termination (Section 40)

  • Reinstatement without loss of benefits.
  • Re-engagement under terms determined by the court/arbitrator.
  • Payment of compensation of not less than 12 months' remuneration.

Topic 8: Dispute Settlement Machinery & Labour Institutions

Categories of Disputes

  • Disputes of Right (Complaints): Arise from the interpretation or enforcement of statutory rights, employment contracts, or CBAs.
  • Disputes of Interest: Arise from demands for new or improved terms and conditions of employment (e.g., wage increases during CBA negotiations).

Dispute Resolution Workflow

  1. Mediation (Commission for Mediation and Arbitration - CMA): Referral to the CMA under Section 86 within 30 days of the dispute arising. The mediator attempts an amicable settlement within 30 days.
  2. Arbitration (CMA): If mediation fails, disputes of right or essential service disputes proceed to binding arbitration under Section 87.
  3. Adjudication (Labour Court): The High Court (Labour Division) hears appeals, revisions, and applications arising from CMA arbitral awards (Section 50, LIA).

Key Labour Institutions (Labour Institutions Act)

  • Labour Economic and Social Council (LESCO): Advisory body on national labour policies, reforms, and ILO matters (Section 3, LIA).
  • Commission for Mediation and Arbitration (CMA): Independent statutory body tasked with mediating and arbitrating labour disputes (Section 12, LIA).
  • Wage Boards: Conduct investigations and recommend minimum wage structures to the Minister (Section 35, LIA).
  • Essential Services Committee: Designates and regulates essential service sectors where strikes/lockouts are restricted (Section 29, LIA).
  • Labour Inspectorate: Ensures compliance with workplace legal standards through inspections and compliance orders (Sections 43–49, LIA).

Topic 9: Workers' Compensation & Social Security

Social security and workers' compensation provide economic protection against loss of income resulting from employment injury, occupational disease, invalidity, retirement, or death.

Core Social Security Benefits

  • Retirement Pension: Benefit accessible upon reaching statutory retirement age with qualifying contribution history (e.g., PSSF Act).
  • Invalidity Benefit: Paid to employees suffering permanent incapacity caused by work-related injury or illness.
  • Survivors' Benefit: Payable to registered dependents upon the death of a covered employee.
  • Maternity & Sickness Benefits: Periodic financial relief during certified periods of medical leave.
  • Employment Injury Compensation: Governed under the Workers' Compensation Act [Cap. 263] for occupational accidents and diseases.
