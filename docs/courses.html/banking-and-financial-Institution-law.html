BANKING & FINANCIAL INSTITUTIONS LAW Sheria ya Benki na Taasisi za Fedha - Tanzania Mainland

Regulatory Framework, Banker-Customer Relationship & Compliance

Topic 1: Regulatory Framework & Role of Bank of Tanzania (BOT)

Banking law in Tanzania Mainland is governed primarily by The Banking and Financial Institutions Act (BFIA), Cap. 342 alongside The Bank of Tanzania Act, Cap. 197.

Primary Roles of the Bank of Tanzania (BOT):

  • Central Banking & Monetary Policy: Formulating and executing monetary policy, issuing legal tender currency, and managing official foreign reserves.
  • Licensing and Supervision: Regulating, licensing, and supervising all banks, financial institutions, and microfinance entities operating in Tanzania.
  • Lender of Last Resort: Providing liquidity support to commercial banks facing temporary liquidity distress to maintain financial stability.
📌 Banking and Financial Institutions Act, Cap. 342 [R.E. 2019] - Section 4
"The Bank of Tanzania shall have the primary responsibility to license, regulate and supervise banks and financial institutions to maintain a sound and stable financial system."

Topic 2: Licensing Requirements, Governance & Capital Adequacy

No person or corporate entity is permitted to conduct banking business or financial business in Tanzania without a valid license issued by the Bank of Tanzania.

1. Licensing and Minimum Capital Requirements

Under Section 8 of Cap. 342, applicants must fulfill strict criteria including proof of minimum capital requirements (e.g., core capital thresholds set by BOT regulations), sound business plans, and corporate governance standards.

2. "Fit and Proper Person" Test (Section 11)

All directors, significant shareholders, and executive officers of a bank must pass BOT's "fit and proper" assessment regarding their integrity, competence, financial soundness, and professional background before taking office.

📌 Banking and Financial Institutions Act, Cap. 342 - Section 16
Banks are legally required to maintain minimum capital adequacy ratios and unimpaired capital reserves to cushion against operational and credit risks.

Topic 3: The Banker-Customer Relationship & Duty of Confidentiality

The legal relationship between a bank and its customer is primarily a contractual relationship based on debtor and creditor dynamics (where the bank becomes a debtor to the customer for money deposited).

1. Nature of the Relationship

  • Debtor / Creditor: Money deposited becomes the property of the bank, which is under a contractual duty to repay upon demand.
  • Principal / Agent: When the bank executes orders on behalf of the customer (e.g., clearing checks or executing wire transfers).

2. Duty of Confidentiality (Ukimya wa Kibenki)

The bank owes a strict contractual and statutory duty of confidentiality to preserve the privacy of customer financial records and transactions.

⚖️ Precedent Case: Tournier v. National Provincial and Union Bank of England [1924] 1 KB 461
Kesi hii mashuhuri ilithibitisha wajibu wa benki kutunza siri za mteja (Duty of Confidentiality) na kuainisha mazingira manne tu ya kisheria ambapo benki inaruhusiwa kutoa taarifa za mteja:
1. Iwapo kuna amri ya kisheria (Under compulsion of law).
2. Iwapo kuna wajibu kwa umma kutoa taarifa hiyo (Duty to the public).
3. Iwapo maslahi ya benki yenyewe yanahitaji kutoa taarifa hiyo (Interests of the bank).
4. Iwapo mteja mwenyewe ametoa ridhaa ya wazi au ya bima (Express or implied consent of customer).

Topic 4: Anti-Money Laundering (AML) & Financial Crime Compliance

Banks operate as primary entry points for global commerce and are heavily regulated to prevent financial crimes, money laundering, and terrorism financing.

📌 Anti-Money Laundering Act, Cap. 423 [R.E. 2019] - Section 15
Banks and financial institutions are classified as "reporting entities" and must enforce mandatory compliance obligations under the oversight of the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU).

Mandatory Statutory AML Duties:

  • Know Your Customer (KYC): Verifying the identity of customers, beneficial owners, and physical addresses before opening accounts.
  • Suspicious Transactions Reporting (STR): Mandatory reporting of any transaction suspected to involve illicit proceeds or terrorism financing to the FIU.
  • Record Keeping: Maintaining transaction records and customer identification data for a statutory minimum period (usually 5 to 10 years).
